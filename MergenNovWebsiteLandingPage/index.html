01The backlog

09:02. Order entry goes down for 340 sales reps.

Thousands of tickets a month arrive by email, portal, phone, chat, monitoring and walk-up. The clearest way to show what Mergen does is to follow one of them. This is INC0048213.

Mon 09:02

One rep emails the help desk. Another phones. A third uses the old portal. That makes three records of one problem, and none of them know about the others.

Mon 11:40

The service desk can't tell which server runs order entry. That knowledge lives in a spreadsheet nobody has opened since the last reorganisation.

Tue 10:15

App team. Infrastructure. The hosting vendor. Back to the app team. Every handoff starts the explanation again, and the clock keeps running while the ticket sits in each queue.

Wed 15:10

Someone finally finds it: an expired certificate. The fix takes twelve minutes. Finding the person who could make it took two days and six hours.

$2M an hour

That was one ticket in one application. When an outage is high-impact, this is the median cost.

02The call

Then someone asks a better question.

It stops being “who dropped it?” and becomes “why was it so easy to drop?”

How it starts

Most of our work begins like this, with a ticket that should have been easy. The architect who answers your first message is the one who scopes the work and leads the delivery. You aren't passed to a different team once the contract is signed.

03Listen

First we sit where the work happens.

Week 1

We shadow real tickets across your service desk, application owners, infrastructure, security and HR, and sort the whole backlog by where each ticket is stuck. Process documents describe how work is meant to move. We watch how it does.

Week 2

We scan the instance itself: what is configured, what has been customised, what is licensed and never switched on, and what would break in the next upgrade.

The rule

By the end, everyone who touched INC0048213 has told us their part of it. No recommendation is made before the evidence has been reviewed.

04Measure

Then we lay its path out straight and measure it.

Gap analysis

Laid end to end, INC0048213 is 54 hours and 8 minutes long. Twelve of those minutes are work. The rest is waiting, and every wait has a cause.

Scored and sequenced

Each gap gets a name, an owner and a cost. Current usage is reviewed module by module, then every fix is scored on value and effort and put in order.

What you receive

A maturity assessment and gap analysis your leadership can read in ten minutes and defend in a budget meeting.

05Propose

A route you can fund.

The same ticket, redrawn. Five stops where there were nine handoffs.

Roadmap

Three horizons. Fix the route in the first 90 days. Extend it to HR, security and customer service within six months. Make it predictive within twelve.

Business case

Cost, return and payback period, built from your own measured baseline. The value is proven before the funding is requested.

Design rule

Standard configuration before custom code. Every customisation needs a recorded business reason, which is what keeps your upgrades predictable.

Operating model

Who owns the platform, how changes are governed and what your team needs to learn are decided now, and not left until after go-live.

06Build

Built in sprints. Shown every two weeks.

Sprints 1–2

Foundations. A CMDB aligned to the Common Service Data Model, so the platform knows what runs on what. One intake for every channel.

Sprints 3–5

Routing, service maps and the vendor integration. A ticket now reaches the team that owns the service, with its history attached.

Sprints 6–7

Now Assist and AI agents for triage and summaries. Knowledge articles written from resolved incidents. Automated tests for every workflow.

Cutover

User acceptance testing, training and a rehearsed cutover. Migrated records are reconciled against the source, and you see the count.

Beyond IT

The same route extends to HRSD, CSM, SPM, SecOps, GRC and custom apps on App Engine when the work crosses departments.
